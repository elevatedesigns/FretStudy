# FretStudy Curriculum — Part 1: Levels 0–2

*How to read this document:* each lesson lists its **ID**, **thread**, **time**, and **prerequisites**, followed by:
- **Goal**: what the learner can do at the end
- **Explain it like this**: the kid-simple wording (5th-grade level) shown on screen
- **Must-know facts**: the accurate details the lesson must get right
- **Graphics**: what to draw (uses the Visual Language in [01-App-Outline.md §5](01-App-Outline.md))
- **Try it / Play it**: the activity
- **Check it**: the mastery check (passing marks the lesson Completed)

Chord shapes are written **low E → high E**. `x` = don't play, `0` = open string, a number = fret. Finger numbers are in brackets: 1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring, 4 pinky.

---

# LEVEL 0 — Get Ready
**Big goal:** Know your guitar, hold it comfortably, tune it, and read the two most common guitar pictures (chord boxes and tab).
**Time:** about 1 week · **Level Song:** none. The finish line is "Tune all six strings by yourself."

---

### L0.1 — Meet Your Guitar
**Thread:** Technique · **Time:** 8 min · **Prereqs:** none

**Goal:** Name the main parts of a guitar and know the three main kinds.

**Explain it like this:**
> A guitar is a wooden box (or board) with six strings stretched over it. When a string shakes, it makes a sound. The long skinny part is the **neck**. The metal bars across the neck are **frets**. Think of them like stairs: every fret you climb toward the body makes the note one step higher.

**Must-know facts:**
- Parts: headstock, tuning pegs (tuners), nut, neck, fretboard, frets, fret markers (dots at frets 3, 5, 7, 9, 12), body, soundhole (acoustic) or pickups (electric), bridge, saddle, strings.
- Three main types: **steel-string acoustic** (bright, loud, no amp needed), **classical/nylon-string** (soft strings, wide neck, gentle on fingertips), **electric** (thin strings, needs an amp, easiest to press).
- Every lesson works on all three. Lessons note when a technique is mostly for one type (for example, bends are easiest on electric).

**Graphics:**
- A large labeled guitar illustration. Tapping a part highlights it and plays a short sound or animation (tap the strings and they wiggle; tap a tuner and it turns).
- Side-by-side cards for acoustic, classical, and electric, with a "what it sounds like" play button.

**Try it:** "Find it" game: the app names a part and the learner taps it on the picture.
**Check it:** Label 6 of 8 parts correctly.

### L0.2 — Holding the Guitar and the Pick
**Thread:** Technique · **Time:** 10 min · **Prereqs:** L0.1

**Goal:** Sit (or stand) with good posture, hold a pick correctly, and place the fretting hand without squeezing.

**Explain it like this:**
> Hug the guitar like a friend: its back touches your belly and chest. Your fretting hand holds the neck like you're gently holding a baseball, with your thumb behind and your fingers in front. Pinch the pick between your thumb and the side of your pointer finger, with just the tip peeking out.

**Must-know facts:**
- Sitting (right-handed player): the guitar's waist rests on the right leg. The neck points slightly up, not drooping. Shoulders are relaxed.
- Standing: use a strap set so the guitar sits at about the same height as when sitting.
- Pick grip: thumb flat on the pick, side of the index finger underneath, about 1/4 inch (a few millimeters) of tip showing. Too much tip means less control.
- Fretting hand: thumb behind the neck (roughly behind fingers 1 and 2), wrist fairly straight, palm not glued to the neck. Elbow hangs free and isn't pinned to your side.
- **No-tension rule:** if anything hurts or feels tight, stop, shake out, breathe, and use less force.

**Graphics:**
- Posture photos and illustrations for sitting and standing, with green checkmarks on the good points and red X's on common mistakes (neck drooping, wrist bent hard, thumb wrapped too far over).
- Pick-grip close-up in 3 steps (animated).
- Fretting hand diagram with **finger colors** introduced: 1 blue, 2 green, 3 orange, 4 pink-purple.

**Try it:** A posture checklist with side-by-side "good vs. not yet" pictures. The learner checks themselves in a mirror, item by item.
**Check it:** Self-check list of 5 items, plus 2 picture questions ("Which pick grip is correct?").

### L0.3 — String Names, Fret Numbers, Finger Numbers
**Thread:** Notes/Theory · **Time:** 10 min · **Prereqs:** L0.2

**Goal:** Name strings 1–6 and their letters, count frets, and use finger numbers.

**Explain it like this:**
> The strings have numbers *and* names. The skinniest string is #1 and the fattest is #6. From fattest to skinniest, their names are **E A D G B E**. Remember it with **"Eddie Ate Dynamite, Good Bye Eddie."** Frets are counted from the headstock end: the first fret is closest to the tuners. Your fretting fingers are numbered too: pointer = 1, middle = 2, ring = 3, pinky = 4.

**Must-know facts:**
- String 6 = low E (thickest), 5 = A, 4 = D, 3 = G, 2 = B, 1 = high E (thinnest).
- "High" and "low" mean **sound**, not position. The high E string is closest to the floor when you hold the guitar.
- Fret 1 is the space between the nut and the first metal fret wire. Press **just behind** the fret wire (on the nut side), not on top of it.
- Finger numbers: 1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring, 4 pinky (T = thumb, rarely used). The picking hand uses p-i-m-a later (Level 6).

**Graphics:**
- **Neck Map** introduced: high E on top, matching tab. Each string is labeled with its number and letter and colored as you hover over it.
- Animation: a finger presses "just behind fret 3," with a zoomed callout showing the sweet spot vs. too far back vs. on top of the wire.
- Hand with colored finger numbers (reused everywhere).

**Try it:** "Pluck string 4!" games. The microphone listens, or the learner taps the matching string on screen.
**Check it:** 6 quick questions (for example, "What's the name of string 2?" → B; "Which finger is #3?" → ring).

### L0.4 — Tuning Up
**Thread:** Technique/Ear · **Time:** 12 min · **Prereqs:** L0.3

**Goal:** Tune all six strings with the built-in tuner.

**Explain it like this:**
> Tuning is like making sure everyone on a team is wearing the same jersey color. Each string has to match its exact note. Turning the tuning peg makes the string tighter (higher sound) or looser (lower sound). Watch the tuner needle and stop when it's in the middle and turns green.

**Must-know facts:**
- Standard tuning (low to high): **E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4**.
- Tighter string = higher pitch. Turn slowly. If the needle moves the wrong way, turn the other way.
- **Tune up to the note.** If you're too high, go below the note, then come back up. This helps the string hold its tuning.
- Pluck once and let it ring while you turn. Don't keep re-plucking hard.
- Guitars drift out of tune: check every time you play.
- (The "5th-fret" tuning-by-ear method comes later, in L3.10, once learners know notes.)

**Graphics:**
- Live **Tuner** UI: big needle and string selector (shows which peg goes with which string). Colors: red = far off, yellow = close, green = in tune.
- Headstock picture showing **which peg tunes which string**, for both 3+3 and 6-in-a-line headstocks (user picks theirs).

**Try it:** Tune all 6 strings with the app's microphone tuner. Plain-text fallback: use a clip-on tuner.
**Check it:** The app hears all six open strings within ±10 cents.

### L0.5 — Reading Chord Boxes
**Thread:** Chords · **Time:** 8 min · **Prereqs:** L0.3

**Goal:** Read a chord diagram and place fingers from it.

**Explain it like this:**
> A chord box is a picture of the guitar neck standing up, like you're looking at it from the front. The lines going up and down are strings (fat string on the left), and the lines going across are frets. A dot means "put a finger here," and the number in the dot tells you which finger. An **O** on top means "play this string open." An **X** means "don't play this string."

**Must-know facts:**
- Vertical lines = strings (6 on the left → 1 on the right). Horizontal lines = frets. The thick top line = the nut.
- If the chord is higher up the neck, a label like "5fr" shows the starting fret.
- A curved bar = **barre** (one finger presses several strings). Introduced properly in Level 4.

**Graphics:**
- Animated "rotate" from a real neck photo into the chord box so the learner *sees* why the box looks that way.
- Example box: **Em** (`0 2 2 0 0 0`, fingers [2] on string 5 and [3] on string 4), with finger colors.

**Try it:** Read 3 boxes and tap where the fingers go on a Neck Map.
**Check it:** 4 identify-the-dot questions.

### L0.6 — Reading Tab
**Thread:** Notes · **Time:** 8 min · **Prereqs:** L0.3

**Goal:** Read tablature (tab) and play single notes from it.

**Explain it like this:**
> Tab is a treasure map for guitar. It has 6 lines, one for each string, and the **top line is the skinniest string**, just like the Neck Map. The numbers tell you which fret to press. A **0** means play the string open. Read it left to right, like a book.

**Must-know facts:**
- Top line = string 1 (high E). Bottom line = string 6 (low E).
- Numbers stacked straight up and down are played at the same time (a chord).
- Basic tab doesn't show rhythm. FretStudy tab always adds rhythm stems underneath, which are explained in Level 1 and up.

**Graphics:**
- Tab staff lined up directly under a horizontal Neck Map. Each tab number lights up the matching dot (same color) as the playhead moves.

**Try it:** Play a 6-note open-string pattern from tab (`E A D G B E` open, then back down).
**Check it:** 4 questions ("A 3 on the second line from the top means…?" → string 2, fret 3).

### L0.7 — Feeling the Beat
**Thread:** Rhythm · **Time:** 8 min · **Prereqs:** none (can be done anytime in Level 0)

**Goal:** Tap a steady beat with a metronome and count "1 2 3 4."

**Explain it like this:**
> Music has a heartbeat called the **beat**. Most songs count it in groups of four: 1, 2, 3, 4, 1, 2, 3, 4. A **metronome** is a clock that clicks the beat for you. **BPM** means "beats per minute." 60 BPM is one click every second.

**Must-know facts:**
- Beat, tempo, BPM, measure (bar), and 4/4 time ("four beats in each measure").
- Beat 1 is the strongest. The app's metronome accents it.

**Graphics:**
- A pulsing circle, with 4 boxes lighting up on 1-2-3-4. A foot icon taps along.

**Try it:** Tap the screen (or the guitar body) on the beat at 60, then 80 BPM. The app shows a timing meter (early / on time / late).
**Check it:** 8 taps in a row within the on-time window at 70 BPM.

**🏁 Level 0 Check (lesson L0.8 in the app):** A 5-question mixed quiz: string names, a chord box, a tab line, finger numbers, BPM. Tuning all six strings is practiced in L0.4.

# LEVEL 1 — First Strums
**Big goal:** Make real music in week one. Use easy chords (1–3 fingers), steady down-strums, and simple tab riffs.
**Time:** 2–3 weeks · **Level Song:** "Ode to Joy" (Beethoven, public domain): easy C and easy G, plus the melody on strings 1–2

> Why easy chords first? The Great Courses method starts with a 1-finger G and a 2-finger C so beginners play a song in their first sitting. *Alfred's* uses 3-string chords for the same reason. Full versions come in Level 2, once fingers are stronger.

---

### L1.1 — Your First Chord: E Minor
**Thread:** Chords · **Time:** 12 min · **Prereqs:** L0.4, L0.5

**Goal:** Play Em cleanly, plus the 1-finger Em7.

**Explain it like this:**
> A chord is a **team of notes** played together. E minor is a great first chord because it uses all six strings and only two fingers. Minor chords sound a little sad or serious.

**Must-know facts:**
- **Em** = `0 2 2 0 0 0`, fingers [2] on string 5, fret 2 and [3] on string 4, fret 2.
- **Em7 (one finger)** = `0 2 0 0 0 0`, finger [2] on string 5, fret 2. Notes E-B-D-G-B-E. Same family, slightly dreamier.
- Fingertips stand up tall like a little arch so they don't touch the neighbor strings.

**Graphics:**
- Chord boxes for Em and Em7 (finger colors).
- "String check" animation: each string plucked one at a time, showing ✅ or 🔇 (muted). The learner sees which string is buzzing or blocked.
- A fingertip close-up showing the arch.

**Try it:** Pluck each string of Em one by one (find the dead strings), then strum all 6.
**Play it:** Strum Em 4 times, then Em7 4 times, over a slow drum loop.
**Check it:** The microphone detects Em with all 6 strings ringing (or self-check: "every string rings clearly").

---

### L1.2 — Strumming Down on the Beat
**Thread:** Rhythm · **Time:** 10 min · **Prereqs:** L1.1, L0.7

**Goal:** Strum steady **quarter notes** (one down-strum per beat).

**Explain it like this:**
> Strumming is brushing the pick across the strings. Each time the metronome clicks, strum down once: 1, 2, 3, 4. Let your wrist be loose like you're shaking water off your hand. Steady is way more important than fast.

**Must-know facts:**
- Down-strum = pick moves toward the floor. Strum from the wrist and forearm, not by locking the whole arm.
- Strum through the strings. Don't dig in. Pick held loosely enough to "give" a little.
- Quarter note = 1 beat.

**Graphics:**
- **Strum Grid** introduced: `1 2 3 4` with a solid ↓ in each box. The playhead moves across.
- A slow-motion wrist animation (loose vs. stiff).

**Try it:** Em, 4 down-strums per bar at 60 BPM → 70 → 80.
**Check it:** Play 4 bars at 70 BPM with the app's timing meter showing 80%+ on time.

---

### L1.3 — Easy G and Easy C
**Thread:** Chords · **Time:** 15 min · **Prereqs:** L1.2

**Goal:** Play the small versions of G and C, strumming only the top 4 strings.

**Explain it like this:**
> Some chords don't use all six strings, so you aim your strum. Easy G uses one finger! Easy C uses two. Only strum the four skinny strings. Start your strum at string 4.

**Must-know facts:**
- **Easy G** = `x x 0 0 0 3`, finger [3] on string 1, fret 3. Notes D-G-B-G = G major.
- **Easy C** = `x x 2 0 1 0`, finger [2] on string 4, fret 2 and [1] on string 2, fret 1. Notes E-G-C-E = C major (with E on the bottom).
- G and C are **major** chords, which sound bright and happy.

**Graphics:**
- Chord boxes with an `X` on strings 5–6 and a **shaded "strum zone"** over strings 4–1.
- A strum-arrow animation that starts at string 4.

**Try it:** Strum each chord 4 times. Then do G (4 beats) → C (4 beats).
**Check it:** Self-rating plus microphone check of each chord.

---

### L1.4 — Changing Chords Without Stopping
**Thread:** Technique · **Time:** 12 min · **Prereqs:** L1.3

**Goal:** Switch between two chords in time.

**Explain it like this:**
> The hardest part of guitar at first is switching chords without stopping. Trick #1: **look ahead**. Picture the next chord before you move. Trick #2: move **all your fingers together**, like a claw landing. Trick #3: it's OK to strum the open strings on beat 4 while your hand is flying to the next chord. The music keeps going!

**Must-know facts:**
- Practice the *switch* with no strumming first: chord A → chord B → chord A, slowly.
- Lift fingers only a little. Small movements are faster.
- **One-Minute Changes**: count clean switches in 60 seconds and try to beat your score each day.
- "Open-strum on beat 4" is a real technique players use. Rhythm matters more than perfect notes.

**Graphics:**
- **Animated Fretboard**: fingers glide from easy G to easy C, with the path traced in finger colors.
- A One-Minute Changes counter with a personal-best graph.

**Try it:** One-Minute Changes: Em ↔ easy G, and easy G ↔ easy C.
**Check it:** 20+ changes in a minute on one pair, or 8 bars of G-C at 70 BPM without stopping.

### L1.5 — The D7 Chord (Your First "Pulling" Chord)
**Thread:** Chords/Ear · **Time:** 12 min · **Prereqs:** L1.4

**Goal:** Play D7 and hear how it "wants" to go to G.

**Explain it like this:**
> D7 looks like a little triangle pointing at the headstock. It's a "tension" chord: when you play it, your ears want to hear G next. Like a ball thrown up in the air, it has to come back down.

**Must-know facts:**
- **D7** = `x x 0 2 1 2`, fingers [2] on string 3, fret 2; [1] on string 2, fret 1; [3] on string 1, fret 2. Strum the top 4 strings.
- Notes D-A-C-F♯: a **dominant 7th** chord. Its pull toward G is called *resolution*.
- With G, C, and D7 you can play hundreds of songs in the key of G.

**Graphics:**
- D7 chord box with a dotted **triangle** outline.
- A "tension meter" animation: the needle rises on D7 and falls back to "home" on G.

**Try it:** G → D7 → G. Listen for the pull.
**Play it:** A practice progression with quarter-note strums: `G | G | D7 | D7 | G | G | D7 | G`.
**Check it:** Ear question: "Which chord sounds finished: the 1st or the 2nd?" (G vs D7). Plus 8 bars of G↔D7.

### L1.6 — Picking Single Strings and Open-String Riffs
**Thread:** Notes · **Time:** 10 min · **Prereqs:** L0.6, L1.2

**Goal:** Pick individual strings accurately from tab.

**Explain it like this:**
> Strumming is like painting with a big brush. Picking is like drawing with a pencil: one string at a time. Rest your picking hand lightly near the bridge so you don't get lost.

**Must-know facts:**
- Downstrokes only for now (marked ⊓).
- Light anchoring (palm edge near the bridge, or pinky on the body) is OK for accuracy. Keep it light.
- Riffs use open strings 6, 5, and 4 (E, A, D).

**Graphics:**
- Tab plus Neck Map with the moving playhead. Each string glows when it's time to pick it.

**Try it:** Three original open-string riffs. Example in steady quarter notes: `E E A A D D A A`.
**Check it:** Play riff #3 at 70 BPM with 90% accuracy (microphone).

### L1.7 — Your First Fretted Notes: Strings 1 and 2
**Thread:** Notes/Melody · **Time:** 15 min · **Prereqs:** L1.6

**Goal:** Play notes on strings 1–2 in first position and play a real melody.

**Explain it like this:**
> In "first position," finger 1 handles fret 1, finger 2 handles fret 2, and finger 3 handles fret 3. Each finger gets its own fret, like assigned seats! Now you can play melodies, the part people sing.

**Must-know facts:**
- String 1 (E): **E** = open, **F** = fret 1 [1], **G** = fret 3 [3].
- String 2 (B): **B** = open, **C** = fret 1 [1], **D** = fret 3 [3].
- "One finger per fret" in first position.

**Graphics:**
- Neck Map showing the 6 notes in **note-name mode**, with finger colors on the dots.
- Tab with matching colored numbers. A small treble staff appears faded underneath (preview of reading music, optional).

**Play it:** "Ode to Joy" (opening phrase): E E F G | G F E D | C C D E | E D D. Every note is on strings 1–2.
**Check it:** Play the phrase at 60 BPM (microphone ≥ 85% note accuracy).

### L1.8 — Ear Lab: High/Low and Happy/Sad
**Thread:** Theory & Ear · **Time:** 8 min · **Prereqs:** L1.3

**Goal:** Hear the difference between higher and lower notes, and between major and minor chords.

**Explain it like this:**
> Major chords usually sound **bright and happy**, and minor chords sound **darker or sad**. G and C are major. Em is minor. Your ears already know the difference. Let's prove it!

**Must-know facts:**
- Major vs minor is a "feeling" label, and it's real. The exact reason (one note, the **3rd**, is lower in minor) comes in L2.5.

**Graphics:**
- Two faces (☀️ / 🌧) with chord sounds. A cartoon ladder for high/low.

**Try it:** 10 rapid listening questions (which note is higher? major or minor?).
**Check it:** 8 of 10 correct.

### L1.9 — 🎵 Level 1 Song: "Ode to Joy"
**Thread:** Song · **Time:** 20 min · **Prereqs:** L1.5, L1.7, L1.8

**Goal:** Put it all together: chords, steady strumming, and a melody.

**Must-know facts:**
- Key of C. Chords, one per bar: **C, G, C, G, C, G, C, C** (easy C and easy G).
- Part A: strum quarter notes along with the metronome.
- Part B: play the whole melody (both halves). Every note is on strings 1–2: `E E F G | G F E D | C C D E | E. D D` then `E E F G | G F E D | C C D E | D. C C`.

> *Change from the first draft:* the Level 1 song was "Skip to My Lou." Its melody needs notes on string 3 that aren't taught until Level 3, so "Ode to Joy" replaces it. It uses only notes and chords from Level 1, and it continues the melody started in L1.7.

**Graphics:** Chord pair (easy C, easy G), strum grid with a moving playhead, and tab with bar lines and a playhead.

**🏁 Level 1 Check:** Play-along (timing checked by the mic when it's on), plus a 5-question quiz (name Em, D7, and easy C from boxes, read a tab note, major vs minor by ear).

# LEVEL 2 — Open Chord Foundations
**Big goal:** Learn the full open chords every guitarist uses (C, G, D, A, E, Am, Em, Dm), strum eighth notes down-up, and learn notes, half steps, and whole steps.
**Time:** 4–6 weeks (Learn & Master estimates about 3 weeks for open chords alone) · **Level Song:** "Oh, Susanna" (G, C, D)

---

### L2.1 — Clean Chords: The Buzz Detective
**Thread:** Technique · **Time:** 10 min · **Prereqs:** Level 1

**Goal:** Fix buzzing and muted strings on your own.

**Explain it like this:**
> When a string buzzes or goes "thunk," something is in its way. Be a detective: pluck each string one at a time and find the bad one. Then check the four usual suspects: (1) Is your finger right behind the fret? (2) Is it on the tip? (3) Is another finger leaning on it? (4) Are you pressing enough (but not crazy hard)?

**Must-know facts:**
- Most buzzes come from finger placement, not weak hands. Press with just enough pressure. Try pressing lighter until it buzzes, then add a little.
- Sore fingertips are normal in weeks 1–4 (calluses form). Sharp pain is not normal. Stop if it hurts.
- Fretting-hand nails short.

**Graphics:** A "Buzz Detective" checklist with 4 illustrated suspects. A zoomed side view of a finger flattening and touching the next string.

**Try it:** String-by-string check on Em, easy C, and D7.
**Check it:** Diagnose 3 audio and picture examples ("this buzz is caused by…").

---

### L2.2 — Full G and Full C
**Thread:** Chords · **Time:** 15 min · **Prereqs:** L2.1

**Goal:** Upgrade to the full-size G and C.

**Explain it like this:**
> Remember easy G and easy C? Now we add the bass notes, the deep notes, to make them sound big and full.

**Must-know facts:**
- **G** = `3 2 0 0 0 3`, fingers [2] on string 6, fret 3; [1] on string 5, fret 2; [3] on string 1, fret 3. All 6 strings. (Alternate fingering `3 2 0 0 0 3` with [3][2][4] makes a later move to C easier. Show both and let learners choose.)
- **C** = `x 3 2 0 1 0`, fingers [3] on string 5, fret 3; [2] on string 4, fret 2; [1] on string 2, fret 1. Don't play string 6.
- C has a "staircase" look: fingers 1-2-3 go down one fret and down one string each.

**Graphics:** Chord boxes, plus a "grow" animation from the easy versions (new fingers fade in). The C "staircase" is highlighted with a dotted diagonal line.

**Try it / Play it:** One-Minute Changes G ↔ C. Then the progression `G | C | G | D7` with quarter notes.
**Check it:** Microphone check of both chords, plus 20 changes per minute.

---

### L2.3 — D and A
**Thread:** Chords · **Time:** 15 min · **Prereqs:** L2.2

**Goal:** Play D major and A major.

**Explain it like this:**
> D looks like a little arrowhead pointing toward the headstock. A is three fingers lined up shoulder-to-shoulder in one fret, like three kids squeezing onto one bench.

**Must-know facts:**
- **D** = `x x 0 2 3 2`, fingers [1] on string 3, fret 2; [3] on string 2, fret 3; [2] on string 1, fret 2. Strum the top 4.
- **A** = `x 0 2 2 2 0`, fingers on strings 4, 3, 2 at fret 2. Common fingerings: [1][2][3] or [2][1][3]. Strum 5 strings. Make sure string 1 still rings (arch the fingers).

**Graphics:** Chord boxes, with the A "bench" highlighted. A side-by-side of the two A fingerings.

**Play it:** `D | A | D | G` (key of D chord family preview).
**Check it:** Microphone check plus One-Minute Changes D ↔ A (target 20+).

---

### L2.4 — E and Am: Same Shape, New Spot!
**Thread:** Chords · **Time:** 12 min · **Prereqs:** L2.3

**Goal:** Play E major and A minor, and notice they're the same hand shape.

**Explain it like this:**
> Here's a secret: E major and A minor are the **exact same finger shape**. You just slide the whole shape over one string! Learn one, get one free.

**Must-know facts:**
- **E** = `0 2 2 1 0 0`, fingers [2] on string 5, fret 2; [3] on string 4, fret 2; [1] on string 3, fret 1. All 6 strings.
- **Am** = `x 0 2 2 1 0`, fingers [2] on string 4, fret 2; [3] on string 3, fret 2; [1] on string 2, fret 1. Strum 5 strings.

**Graphics:** **Animated Fretboard**: the E shape "slides" one string toward the floor and becomes Am. Both boxes are side by side with matching colors.

**Play it:** `Am | E | Am | E` ("spooky" sound), then `Am | C | G | Am`.
**Check it:** One-Minute Changes Am ↔ E (target 25+, since it's the same shape!).

### L2.5 — Dm, and Why Minor Sounds Sad
**Thread:** Chords + Theory · **Time:** 15 min · **Prereqs:** L2.4, L1.8

**Goal:** Play Dm, and see that major → minor changes just **one note**.

**Explain it like this:**
> Every basic chord has three different notes: the **root** (the chord's name, its home base), the **3rd**, and the **5th**. To turn a major chord into a minor chord, you lower the 3rd by **one fret**. That one tiny move changes happy to sad!

**Must-know facts:**
- **Dm** = `x x 0 2 3 1`, fingers [2] on string 3, fret 2; [3] on string 2, fret 3; [1] on string 1, fret 1.
- One-note changes:
  - **D → Dm**: string 1 goes fret 2 → fret 1 (F♯ → F).
  - **E → Em**: string 3 goes fret 1 → open (G♯ → G).
  - **A → Am**: string 2 goes fret 2 → fret 1 (C♯ → C).
- The note that changes is the **3rd**.

**Graphics:**
- First use of **Note-Role colors** (preview): the root is a vermillion square, the 3rd sky blue, the 5th green. In each pair, only the blue dot moves, with an animated drop of one fret and the face icon switching ☀️ → 🌧.
- A legend box: "We're showing NOTE JOBS now, not fingers."

**Try it:** Toggle between each major/minor pair and listen.
**Check it:** 3 ear questions (major or minor?) plus "Which string changes to turn A into Am?"

### L2.6 — Eighth-Note Strumming: Down-Up
**Thread:** Rhythm · **Time:** 15 min · **Prereqs:** L1.2, L2.2

**Goal:** Strum eighth notes (down on the beat, up on the "and").

**Explain it like this:**
> Now we count **"1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and."** Strum **down** on the numbers and **up** on the "ands." Your hand swings like a pendulum on a clock and never stops. Down, up, down, up. On the up-strum, you only need to catch the top few strings.

**Must-know facts:**
- Eighth note = half a beat. Two eighth notes = one beat.
- Downstrokes always land on numbers. Upstrokes always land on "&."
- Learn & Master's "secret to great strumming": **keep your hand going.**

**Graphics:**
- Strum Grid with 8 boxes `1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &`, with ↓ on numbers and ↑ on "&" (↑ arrows shorter, covering only strings 1–3).
- A pendulum animation synced to the metronome.

**Try it:** Muted strums first (lay your fretting hand lightly across the strings to make a "chk" sound), then G, then `G | C | D | G`.
**Check it:** 4 bars of continuous down-up at 70 BPM, ≥ 80% on time.

### L2.7 — Smooth Switches: Anchor Fingers and Shortcuts
**Thread:** Technique · **Time:** 12 min · **Prereqs:** L2.5

**Goal:** Use common fingers and guide fingers to change faster.

**Explain it like this:**
> Some chords share a finger in the same spot. That finger is an **anchor**: don't lift it, and let the other fingers move around it. Other fingers just slide along the same string. That's a **guide finger**, like a train on its track.

**Must-know facts:**
- **C ↔ Am**: fingers 1 and 2 stay put (anchors). Only finger 3 moves (string 5 fret 3 ↔ string 3 fret 2).
- **Am ↔ E**: the whole shape moves over one string (L2.4).
- **D7 → G** and **D ↔ A**: show guide or shortest paths.
- **G ↔ Em**: with G fingered [2][1][3], finger Em with fingers [1] and [2] (instead of [2] and [3]). Finger 1 then stays on string 5, fret 2 as an anchor.
- The app works out and highlights shared fingers automatically for any chord pair, based on the fingering the learner chose.

**Graphics:** A glowing pulse on anchor fingers. Guide-finger "train track" lines. A "Distance score" shows how many fingers move.

**Try it:** One-Minute Changes on 4 pairs. Record scores.
**Check it:** Beat your L1/L2 personal best on any 2 pairs.

### L2.8 — The Musical Alphabet
**Thread:** Theory · **Time:** 10 min · **Prereqs:** L1.7

**Goal:** Know that music uses letters A–G that repeat, and what an octave is.

**Explain it like this:**
> Music only uses 7 letters: **A B C D E F G**, and then it starts over at A again, just higher. When you get back to the same letter, that's called an **octave**. It sounds like the same note, just higher, like a little kid and a grown-up singing the same song.

**Must-know facts:**
- After G comes A again.
- The low E string and the high E string are both "E," two octaves apart.
- The 12th fret is one octave above the open string (the double dot marker).

**Graphics:** A looping alphabet "ladder" that climbs and repeats. A Neck Map showing open E and 12th-fret E on string 6 linked by a rainbow arc.

**Try it:** Play an open string, then the same string at fret 12. Same note, higher!
**Check it:** 5 questions ("What letter comes after G?" → A; "two letters above F?" → A).

### L2.9 — Half Steps and Whole Steps
**Thread:** Theory · **Time:** 12 min · **Prereqs:** L2.8

**Goal:** Understand that one fret = one half step, and find sharps and flats.

**Explain it like this:**
> Each fret is a **half step**, the smallest step in music. Two frets make a **whole step**. Between most letters there's a "secret note" in the middle called a **sharp (♯)** or **flat (♭)**. Sharp means one fret higher. Flat means one fret lower. But two pairs of letters are already next-door neighbors with no secret note between them: **E–F** and **B–C**.

**Must-know facts:**
- 12 different notes: A, A♯/B♭, B, C, C♯/D♭, D, D♯/E♭, E, F, F♯/G♭, G, G♯/A♭ (then A again).
- A♯ and B♭ are the **same sound** with two names (enharmonic).
- No sharp/flat between E–F and B–C.
- On a piano: no black key between E–F and B–C (a helpful picture).

**Graphics:**
- A **piano keyboard strip lined up above string 6 of the Neck Map**. Each fret lines up with a key. E–F and B–C gaps are highlighted.
- Animated "frog hops": 1 hop = half step, 2 hops = whole step.

**Try it:** On string 1, play E → F (half), F → G (whole). On string 2, play B → C (half), C → D (whole).
**Check it:** 6 questions (count frets for half/whole; name the note one half step above E → F).

### L2.10 — Notes on Strings 1 and 2, Plus Simple Reading
**Thread:** Notes/Melody · **Time:** 15 min · **Prereqs:** L1.7, L2.9

**Goal:** Master all first-position notes on strings 1–2 and (optionally) read them on the staff.

**Must-know facts:**
- String 1: E (0), F (1), G (3). String 2: B (0), C (1), D (3). Same as L1.7, now with names memorized.
- **Optional "Gold Star" reading track:** notes on the treble staff (lines E-G-B-D-F "Every Good Boy Does Fine," spaces F-A-C-E). Whole note = 4 beats, half = 2, quarter = 1. Guitar music sounds an octave lower than written.

**Graphics:** Tab and staff stacked, with each note colored and linked to the Neck Map dot. A flashcard drill with a staff, Neck Map, and name triad.

**Play it:** "Jingle Bells" chorus melody in the key of C. Every note (E, F, G, C, D) is on strings 1–2. A backing track plays the chords. Strumming this song waits until the learner knows F (L4.6).
**Check it:** Note-name flashcards (8/10) plus melody at 70 BPM.

### L2.11 — 🎵 Level 2 Song: "Oh, Susanna"
**Thread:** Song · **Time:** 20 min · **Prereqs:** L2.6, L2.7

**Must-know facts:**
- Key of G. Chords **G, C, D** (full shapes), with eighth-note down-up strumming.
- Bonus: swap D for D7 at the end of lines to hear the "pull."

**Graphics:** Song chart with chord boxes and the strum grid. "Danger zones" (fast changes) marked with a ⚡ and given a loop button.

**🏁 Level 2 Check:**
- Play: the song at 80 BPM with steady eighth notes (≥ 80% timing, chords detected).
- Quiz (8 questions): name chords from boxes (E, A, D, Dm, Am, C, G), major vs minor ear, half/whole step counting, musical alphabet.
- Self-rate: "I can change between G, C, and D without stopping."
