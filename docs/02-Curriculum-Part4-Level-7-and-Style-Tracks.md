# LEVEL 7 — Harmony & Expression
**Big goal:** Understand *why* music works well enough to play over chord changes, use color chords, hear songs and figure them out, and write your own music.
**Time:** 8+ weeks · **Level Song:** "My Song." The capstone is an original 2-section song (verse and chorus) with a recorded rhythm part and a solo over it.

---

### L7.1 — Seventh Chords: The Four Main Flavors
**Thread:** Theory · **Time:** 15 min · **Prereqs:** L4.12, L6.6

**Explain it like this:**
> Take a triad (1-3-5) and stack one more note on top, the 7th. There are four main flavors: **major 7** (dreamy), **dominant 7** (bluesy, wants to move), **minor 7** (smooth and cool), and **half-diminished** (mysterious). Every key has all of them in a set order.

**Must-know facts:**
- Formulas: **maj7** = 1-3-5-7 · **7 (dominant)** = 1-3-5-♭7 · **m7** = 1-♭3-5-♭7 · **m7♭5 (half-diminished)** = 1-♭3-♭5-♭7. Fully **diminished 7** = 1-♭3-♭5-♭♭7.
- **Diatonic 7th chords** in every major key: **Imaj7, ii7, iii7, IVmaj7, V7, vi7, viiø7**. In C: Cmaj7, Dm7, Em7, Fmaj7, G7, Am7, Bm7♭5.
- Only the **V chord** is naturally a dominant 7. That's why it pulls home so strongly.

**Graphics:** A stacked-thirds tower with the 7th (purple) added on top. A 4-flavor card set with sound buttons. The Number Wheel upgraded to 7th chords.
**Check it:** Spell 4 chords from formulas (for example, Dm7 = D F A C), plus an ear test: maj7 vs dominant 7.

### L7.2 — Movable 7th Chord Shapes
**Thread:** Chords · **Time:** 20 min · **Prereqs:** L7.1, L4.8

**Must-know facts:**
- **Root on string 6** (example at fret 1, root F; string 5 muted):
  - maj7 `1 x 2 2 1 x` · 7 `1 x 1 2 1 x` · m7 `1 x 1 1 1 x`
- **Root on string 5** (example at fret 3, root C):
  - maj7 `x 3 5 4 5 3` · 7 `x 3 5 3 5 3` · m7 `x 3 5 3 4 3` · m7♭5 `x 3 4 3 4 x`
- **m7♭5 with root on string 6** (example root A at fret 5): `5 x 5 5 4 x`.
- These are the backbone of jazz, soul, and R&B rhythm guitar (Learn & Master Session 18; *Beginning Jazz Guitar* Ch. 3).

**Graphics:** Chord boxes in note-role colors (R/3/5/7 labeled). A "flavor switch" animation shows which single note moves between maj7 → 7 → m7.
**Play it:** `Cmaj7 | Am7 | Dm7 | G7` (a 1–6–2–5 progression) using both root strings, staying within 4 frets.
**Check it:** Play 6 called-out 7th chords.

### L7.3 — Chord-Tone Soloing and Arpeggios
**Thread:** Lead · **Time:** 20 min · **Prereqs:** L7.1, L6.7, L5.10

**Explain it like this:**
> When the chord changes, the "safest" and most musical notes are the ones **inside the chord**: its root, 3rd, 5th, and 7th. Landing on the chord's **3rd** right when the chord changes makes your solo sound like it knows exactly where the song is going.

**Must-know facts:**
- **Arpeggio** = a chord's notes played one at a time.
- Major and minor triad arpeggios, then 7th arpeggios, inside the CAGED shapes (*Guitar Scales in Context* shows each scale shape with its triad and 7th arpeggio).
- Learn & Master's soloing rule: "Let the chord tones guide you."
- Target practice: play a scale, but land on the 3rd of each new chord on beat 1.

**Graphics:** The scale shown in gray with the current chord's tones lighting up in color as the backing track changes chords ("chord tones follow the music" view).
**Try it:** Solo over `Am | Dm | E7 | Am` targeting the 3rds (C, F, G♯, C).
**Check it:** A target drill: the app checks if beat 1 of each bar lands on a chord tone.

### L7.4 — Mixing Major and Minor Pentatonic over the Blues
**Thread:** Lead · **Time:** 15 min · **Prereqs:** L6.2, L5.13

**Must-know facts:**
- Over a blues in A: major pentatonic (A B C♯ E F♯) sounds sweet, and minor pentatonic (A C D E G) sounds gritty. Mix them, especially the **♭3 → 3 slide (C → C♯)**.
- *Spotlight Blues Guitar*: use major tones over the I chord, and switch to minor pentatonic over the IV chord.
- Major 6th = sweeter. ♭7 = bluesier.

**Explain it like this:**
> Blues players use both the happy ladder and the sad ladder at once. That crunchy rub between them is the blues sound.

**Graphics:** Both pentatonics overlaid. Shared notes shown in neutral color, major-only notes in sky blue, minor-only notes in blue.
**Check it:** A solo chorus using at least one ♭3 → 3 move (recorded self-review).

### L7.5 — Modes: Same Notes, New Mood
**Thread:** Theory/Lead · **Time:** 25 min (two sittings) · **Prereqs:** L6.1, L6.7

**Explain it like this:**
> Remember relative minor: same notes, different home? Modes take that idea all the way. Start the major scale from each of its 7 notes and you get 7 different **moods**. You don't need all of them. Three are used constantly: **Dorian** (cool minor), **Mixolydian** (bluesy major), and **Aeolian** (natural minor).

**Must-know facts:**
| Mode | Formula vs major | Mood | Common use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ionian | 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 | Happy | Pop, folk (the major scale) |
| Dorian | 1 2 ♭3 4 5 **6** ♭7 | Cool, hopeful minor | Funk, jazz, rock (minor with a bright 6) |
| Phrygian | 1 **♭2** ♭3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7 | Spanish, dark | Flamenco, metal |
| Lydian | 1 2 3 **♯4** 5 6 7 | Dreamy, floating | Film music, fusion |
| Mixolydian | 1 2 3 4 5 6 **♭7** | Bluesy major | Blues, rock, country (over dominant chords) |
| Aeolian | 1 2 ♭3 4 5 ♭6 ♭7 | Sad, natural minor | Rock ballads, pop |
| Locrian | 1 ♭2 ♭3 4 ♭5 ♭6 ♭7 | Unstable | Rarely used as a home |

- Teach modes by **sound over a drone or vamp** (*Guitar Scales in Context*: three licks and a backing track for each mode), not by memorizing 7 starting notes.
- The "characteristic note" (bold) is what gives each mode its color.

**Graphics:** A mode "color wheel" with the characteristic note glowing. A drone player with a mode picker. Neck Maps in the parallel approach (all modes from the same root, such as A) so learners hear the difference.
**Try it:** Improvise over an A drone in A Dorian, then A Mixolydian.
**Check it:** Ear test: Dorian vs Aeolian, and Mixolydian vs Ionian (6 of 8).

### L7.6 — Minor Keys and the Harmonic Minor Secret
**Thread:** Theory · **Time:** 15 min · **Prereqs:** L7.5, L4.13

**Must-know facts:**
- Natural minor chords: **i, ii°, ♭III, iv, v, ♭VI, ♭VII**. In A minor: Am, B°, C, Dm, Em, F, G.
- Songs in minor keys usually make the **v chord major (V)** for a stronger pull home (E instead of Em in A minor). That comes from the **harmonic minor** scale (raised 7th: G♯).
- Callback: this is why "House of the Rising Sun" has an E major (L4.13).
- Three minor scales: natural, harmonic, melodic (*Guitar Exercises For Dummies*, Ch. 5).

**Explain it like this:**
> In minor songs, the 5 chord often borrows one "happy" note to push harder toward home.

**Graphics:** A side-by-side Number Wheel (major key vs relative minor key). The G → G♯ change animates.
**Check it:** 5 questions plus ear: "v or V?"

### L7.7 — Color Chords: 6, add9, 9, sus, 11, 13
**Thread:** Chords · **Time:** 20 min · **Prereqs:** L7.2

**Must-know facts:**
- **Color tone** = an extra scale note added to a chord. The 9th = the 2nd, the 11th = the 4th, the 13th = the 6th (an octave up) (Learn & Master Session 18).
- Substitution tricks from Learn & Master Session 12: spice up a **major** chord with an **add9 / "2" chord**, and a **minor** chord with a **minor 11**.
- Common shapes: C6, Cmaj9, Am9, C9, A13 (shown with root on strings 5 and 6).
- **Reading any chord symbol** (Learn & Master Session 20): find the major scale of the root, then apply the formula (for example, Cm7 = 1-♭3-5-♭7 = C E♭ G B♭). If you don't know a chord: **build it, look it up, or simplify it** (drop the color tones).

**Graphics:** A chord-symbol decoder widget: type "F♯m11" and see the formula, the notes, 2 shapes, and the sound.
**Check it:** Decode 5 chord symbols plus play 3 color chords in a progression.

### L7.8 — Outside the Key: Secondary Dominants, Borrowed Chords, Walking Bass
**Thread:** Theory · **Time:** 20 min · **Prereqs:** L7.6, L3.2

**Must-know facts:**
- **Secondary dominant**: a dominant 7 chord that pulls to a chord other than I. For example, in C, **A7 → Dm** (V of ii) and **E7 → Am** (V of vi). (*Mastering Electric Blues Guitar* Ch. 11.)
- **Borrowed chords** from the parallel minor: **♭VII** (B♭ in C), **iv** (Fm in C). Both are very common in rock and pop.
- **Slash chords and walking bass**: connect chords with step-wise bass notes (C → C/B → Am → Am/G → F).
- The Number System still works: write "♭7" or "5/2" (*Music Theory for the Music Industry*: chords outside the key are named relative to the key).

**Explain it like this:**
> Songs sometimes borrow a chord from outside the family for a surprise, like inviting a cousin to the party.

**Graphics:** The Number Wheel with "guest" chords shown in a dashed outline and arrows to their targets. A walking bass line drawn as stairs.
**Check it:** Find the "guest chord" in 4 progressions.

### L7.9 — Figuring Out Songs by Ear
**Thread:** Ear · **Time:** 20 min (repeatable) · **Prereqs:** L6.12, L5.11

**Must-know facts:**
- Process: (1) find the **key** by humming the home note and matching it on string 6 or 5; (2) find the **bass notes**; (3) guess chords from the key's family (1, 4, 5, 6 first); (4) check by playing along; (5) loop hard spots at slower speed (*Guitar Tips*: break songs into parts, loop trouble spots, don't practice at full speed).
- Melodic ear training: sing, then find it (*Train Your Ear*: melodic and harmonic ear-training sections).
- More intervals: major/minor 6th ("My Bonnie Lies Over the Ocean" = major 6th), minor 7th, tritone, minor 2nd.

**Graphics:** A song "detective board": a slow-down player with A–B loop, a key finder, and Number Wheel guessing tiles.
**Check it:** Transcribe a 4-chord progression and an 8-note melody from app audio.

### L7.10 — Expression: Dynamics, Articulation, Phrasing
**Thread:** Technique/Musicianship · **Time:** 15 min · **Prereqs:** L5.10

**Must-know facts:**
- **Dynamics**: soft (p) to loud (f). Change pick attack and strum depth.
- **Articulation**: legato (smooth) vs staccato (short), accents, muting, ghost notes.
- **Phrasing**: treat phrases like sentences with punctuation. **Sing what you play.** Don't treat all phrases the same (*Guitar Tips*, Phrasing chapter).
- **Record yourself** and listen back.

**Explain it like this:**
> Playing the right notes is like reading words correctly. Expression is reading them with feeling, like an actor.

**Graphics:** A waveform "loudness ribbon" under the tab. Articulation icons.
**Check it:** Play one 8-bar passage 3 ways (soft, loud, staccato). The app compares loudness and note lengths.

---

### L7.11 — Write Your Own Song
**Thread:** Songwriting · **Time:** 30 min (project) · **Prereqs:** L4.12, L6.12

**Must-know facts:**
- Pick a key and a progression using numbers. Make the **verse** and **chorus** contrast (different progression, rhythm, or register).
- Write a melody from the pentatonic or major scale. End phrases on chord tones.
- Song form: verse–chorus–verse–chorus–bridge–chorus.
- Start simple. Use a capo to fit your voice (*Guitar Tips*: Songwriting chapter; acoustic beginner books, writing chapters).

**Graphics:** A **Song Builder**: drag Number Wheel tiles into section lanes, pick a strum pattern, hear a playback with drums, and export a chord chart (PDF).
**Check it:** Complete an 8-bar verse plus an 8-bar chorus in the builder.

### L7.12 — 🎵 Level 7 Capstone: "My Song"
Record (in the app) your original song's rhythm part, then a solo over one section using chord tones (L7.3). The recording is saved on the learner's computer and can be exported as a WAV file to share however they like.

**🏁 Level 7 Check:** Quiz (10 questions): 7th chord formulas and diatonic 7ths, modes by formula and sound, minor key V, decode chord symbols, find the guest chord; plus the capstone recording submitted.

# Appendix — Content Rules for Writing Lessons

1. **One new idea per lesson.** If a lesson needs two new ideas, split it.
2. **"Explain it like this" boxes** target a **5th-grade reading level** (check with a Flesch-Kincaid tool: grade ≤ 5.5). Use short sentences, everyday comparisons (stairs, teams, ladders, families, trains), and define every new word in **bold** the first time.
3. **Use only known material.** Songs and exercises use only chords and skills from prerequisite lessons. The Songbook tagging system enforces this.
4. **Every lesson has a graphic, and the graphic has sound.** Diagrams are generated from data by the shared renderer.
5. **Color is never the only signal.** Every dot carries a finger number, note name, or interval label.
6. **Accuracy review.** Every chord shape, note, and formula is checked by a second reviewer (and automatically by the renderer: it computes note names from fret data and flags mismatches with the lesson text).
7. **Music before theory.** Theory lessons always connect back to something the learner already plays ("Remember the E7 → A pull? Here's why.").
8. **Public-domain or original music only** in the app.
9. **Practice prescription.** Every lesson ends with: "Practice this for X minutes a day for Y days," with Learn & Master–style time estimates.
10. **Kind feedback.** Checks never say "wrong." They say "Not yet. Here's what to try," with a direct link back to the step that teaches it.
